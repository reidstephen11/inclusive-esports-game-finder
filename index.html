About this tool

This finder grew out of a Para Esports competition that brought together students with disability from four schools to play Mario Kart 8 Deluxe on Nintendo Switch. The day was not really about the game — it was about social connection, communication, teamwork, friendly competition and students feeling like they belonged. The right game choice is what made that possible.

How the recommendations work

Nothing here is a simple filter. Every game carries weighted scores against each criterion in the finder. Your answers become weights, and each game earns a percentage match:

  • Platform — weighted heaviest. A perfect game you cannot run is not a recommendation.
  • Primary goal and student needs — weighted next, because they drive whether students can actually take part.
  • Budget, group size, game style and event type — weighted lower, as practical fit.

Games are then split into your best match, excellent alternatives and honourable mentions, and each card explains the specific reasons it scored well for your answers.

The department position on game titles

Every card shows where the game sits against the Queensland Department of Education's assessed list:

  • Permitted for use — assessed and permitted, subject to local decision making by the school.
  • Not yet assessed — not on the department's list. Several of the strongest inclusive titles sit here (Minecraft, Overcooked! 2, Moving Out). They are not prohibited: self-assess them against the department position using the flowchart in the Esports in Schools QLearn course.
  • Not permitted — Call of Duty, Counter-Strike 2 and Rainbow Six. These are excluded from this database entirely and can never be recommended by the finder.

The Department-permitted only toggle on the results and All games screens is on by default, so you only ever see assessed titles unless you deliberately widen the search. Turn it off to include the not-yet-assessed games your school can self-assess — the setting is remembered on that device. The department's permitted list also includes titles this database deliberately leaves out — Fortnite, VALORANT, League of Legends and similar — because they are a poor fit for the inclusive settings this tool is built for. Searching for one will tell you its status.

The lists are stored in DEPT_PERMITTED and DEPT_NOT_PERMITTED near the top of the file; update them when the department publishes a revision and every card re-flags itself automatically.

Using it at your school

  • Treat the "suggested school settings" as your starting configuration — they remove most of the skill gap before the first round is played.
  • Run a practice round that does not count. Confidence is built before the competition starts.
  • Mixed-ability teams beat individual ladders for most inclusive events.
  • Check your school's device, network and classification policies before purchasing.

Accuracy and reuse

Each card shows the publisher's own promotional key art, stored in the images folder beside this file so the app still works with no internet connection. Those images remain the property of their respective publishers and are used here only to identify the game — they are not covered by the permission to reuse this tool. If that does not suit your school, delete the images folder: every card falls back to the original vector illustration drawn for this tool, and nothing else breaks.

Prices are indicative Australian recommended retail prices and change often, especially during sales. Age guidance is a classroom recommendation, not a formal classification. This tool is free to copy, adapt and host at your own school — it is a single HTML file with no tracking, no accounts and no internet connection required once saved.
